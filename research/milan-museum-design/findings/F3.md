# F3: Frames, light, and low-label aesthetics — external evidence for/against digital gilt frames, image-adaptive wall color, hidden wall labels, and cultural-institution typography

## Findings

### [1] Major museum websites present collection images without CSS gilt-frame chrome; physical frames are catalogued as separate objects or optional “Framed” views, not as default UI chrome.
- quote: "Framed" … "Frame 17th-century Italian Frame"
- url: https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/vincent-van-gogh-sunflowers
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (page live 2026-09; exhibition history includes 2026 loans)
- confidence: high

### [2] Rijksmuseum treats gilded frames as distinct collection objects linked from the painting record, reinforcing that gilt is documentation of the physical hang, not a digital decorative default.
- quote: "Vergulde lijst met pâte-ornamenten in Empire stijl. (object | former frame)" … "Profiellijst (object | current frame)"
- url: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/The-Milkmaid--42dd0e658c2979aec8e144d2357c55c0
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (object record; provenance through 1908 purchase)
- confidence: high

### [3] National Gallery London collection grids always surface wall-label analogues—title, artist, interpretive snippet, and physical room—so institutional web practice is against a fully wordless hang.
- quote: "A Young Woman standing at a Virginal" / "Johannes Vermeer" / "The young woman at the keyboard holds our eye with a direct gaze…" / "Room 25"
- url: https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/must-sees
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (live 2026-09; © 2016–2026)
- confidence: high

### [4] The National Gallery offers dual collection viewing modes—“full image” vs overview/list with overview—supporting image-first browsing while still not abandoning labels institutionally.
- quote: "Viewing options — Show full image — Show with overview Show list with overview"
- url: https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/explore-the-collection
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (live 2026-09)
- confidence: high

### [5] The Met’s own gallery photography documents the physical precedent the Milan design evokes: oil paintings in ornate gold frames on a colored (blue) institutional wall—not adaptive per-image wall recoloring.
- quote: "Art gallery interior featuring oil paintings of interior scenes in ornate gold frames, with a blue wall and wooden flooring."
- url: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (live 2026-09; © 2000–2026)
- confidence: high

### [6] Rijksmuseum’s online collection landing is image-first (IIIF tiles + title as link text) with no digital gilt frames and no evidence of wall-color sampling from the artwork; institutional digital hang stays visually neutral around the image.
- quote: "The Milkmaid" / "Still Life with Flowers in a Glass Vase" / "The Night Watch Militia Company of District II…"
- url: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (live 2026-09)
- confidence: high

### [7] Tate’s Digital team states an “audience first” content strategy and user-centred research—evidence that major cultural institutions invest in content, accessibility, and journeys, not purely in a low-label visual hang.
- quote: "Our 'audience first' content strategy helps us to realise our ambition to produce quality content that builds scale, particularly for non-specialist audiences." … "The team works with user-centred practices (including quantitative and qualitative research) to ensure that development done serves evidenced user needs."
- url: https://www.tate.org.uk/about-us/digital
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (live 2026-09; awards listed through 2012)
- confidence: high

### [8] Nielsen Norman Group (2025) argues decorative UI that covers or competes with content hurts usability; the content—not the chrome—should take the spotlight, which supports keeping glows/frames off the artwork and out of the way of labels when present.
- quote: "It's like the interface is shouting 'look at me' when it should quietly step aside and let the real star — the content — take the spotlight." … "iOS 26’s visual language obscures content instead of letting it take the spotlight."
- url: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025-10-10
- confidence: high

### [9] W3C WAI’s alt decision tree (updated 2024) requires meaningful text alternatives for informative images that contribute meaning to the page; hiding visual wall labels is only accessibility-safe if equivalent text exists elsewhere (alt, detail view, or metadata).
- quote: "Does the image contribute meaning to the current page or context? Yes: … and it's a simple graphic or photograph. Use a brief description of the image in a way that conveys that meaning in the alt attribute."
- url: https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/decision-tree/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-05-13
- confidence: high

### [10] NN/g (2026) still requires long-form copy for complex content, trust, and SEO/AEO—cost evidence against total wordlessness if the photo wall is meant to be discoverable or educational beyond pure contemplation.
- quote: "With attention-spans dwindling, why do we still use long-form copy? Because some things are complex, require trust, and need SEO & AEO benefits."
- url: https://www.nngroup.com/videos/we-still-need-long-form-copy/
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-08-31
- confidence: medium

### [11] Louvre’s online collections present as a large metadata database (500,000+ entries) with typology facets and titled themed albums—further counter-evidence that institutional digital practice centers structured labels, not low-label visual walls.
- quote: "The Collections database consists of entries for more than 500,000 works in the Musée du Louvre and Musée National Eugène-Delacroix."
- url: https://collections.louvre.fr/en/
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (live 2026-09; albums include “Acquisitions made in 2024”)
- confidence: high

### [12] Across Met, National Gallery, Rijksmuseum, Tate, and Louvre pages fetched for this angle, none sample the current artwork to recolor a “gallery wall” or ship CSS multi-layer gilt box-shadow frames as the default collection presentation—image-adaptive wall color + digital gilt frames are aesthetic inventions relative to 2024–2026 institutional web practice, even though colored walls + gold frames are real physical-gallery conventions.
- quote: (cross-site observation from fetched primary pages; no institutional page documents adaptive wall sampling or CSS gilt as default UI)
- url: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (synthesis of fetched primary museum sites, 2026-09)
- confidence: medium
