# F1: Digital museum / online exhibition design — foyer→hall→artwork narrative, dark vs light gallery UI, restrained navigation, artwork-first vs institution-info-first (prefer 2024–2026)

## Findings

### [1] Major museum homepages in 2026 still open as an institutional foyer (hours/tickets/plan-visit) before any collection surface; artwork-first experiences live on separate Collection/Art URLs.
- quote: "Welcome to New York City's most iconic museum. [Buy tickets] [Plan your visit] ... Open today 10 am–9 pm ... Now On View"
- url: https://www.metmuseum.org/
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19 (site footer © 2000–2026)
- confidence: high

### [2] Rijksmuseum’s homepage CTA is explicitly visit/ticket-first, with masterworks named as bait rather than hung as a gallery; Collection is a peer nav item, not the foyer.
- quote: "Visit the highlights. Book your ticket today. Home of The Night Watch, Home of The Milkmaid, Home of Dutch Masterpieces"
- url: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19
- confidence: high

### [3] Louvre’s homepage foyer is hybrid: open-hours/ticket banner, then an image-led “Highlights” carousel (exhibitions, visitor trails, palace), then a “Delve into the Louvre” artwork grid — institution logistics first, artworks immediately after.
- quote: "Welcome to the Louvre / The museum is open today / 9:00 AM à 6:00 PM / Book a ticket / Prepare your visit ... ## Highlights ... ## Delve into the Louvre"
- url: https://www.louvre.fr/en
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19 (highlights dated 2026-06/2026-07 assets)
- confidence: high

### [4] MoMA’s homepage pairs a short institutional welcome with a large Exhibitions strip, then a secondary “Art and artists in our collection” grid of named works (Matisse, Van Gogh, etc.) — art is present on the foyer but subordinate to visit + exhibitions.
- quote: "Welcome / Explore art and ideas at MoMA. The museum is open 10:30 a.m.–8:30 p.m. today. Plan your visit ... ## Exhibitions ... ## Art and artists in our collection / Discover artworks that speak to you, from the 1880s to today. [Find your favorites]"
- url: https://www.moma.org/
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19
- confidence: high

### [5] Across Met, Tate, Rijksmuseum, Louvre, and MoMA (2026 live sites), top-level navigation is restrained to roughly 4–6 labels: Visit, Exhibitions/What’s On, Art/Collection, Learn/Research, Shop, Support/Membership — no deep mega-menus on the public chrome.
- quote: "Visit / Exhibitions and Events / Art / Learn with Us / Research / Shop" (Met); "What's On / Visit / Art / Learn / Shop" (Tate); "Visit and tickets / What's on / Collection / Stories / Support us / Giftshop / Research" (Rijksmuseum)
- url: https://www.tate.org.uk/visit
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19
- confidence: high

### [6] Dedicated collection surfaces implement a clear foyer→hall→artwork narrative: Discover/theme hubs (hall) → image grids of works (hall browsing) → object pages with large primary image + curatorial metadata + related works (artwork).
- quote: "Explore highlights from our collection of over 70,000 artworks ... ## Art on display / Choose from a selection of popular artworks in our galleries to find out its location"
- url: https://www.tate.org.uk/art
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19
- confidence: high

### [7] Rijksmuseum Collection Online documents an explicit physical-room metaphor online — “My Gallery of Honour” is a virtual space inspired by the museum’s iconic room, plus Comparator and AI Art Explorer — validating spatial/gallery metaphors as current museum digital practice, not a private-site quirk.
- quote: "Visitors are also welcomed into *My Gallery of Honour*, a virtual space inspired by the Rijksmuseum's most iconic room. Curate your own Gallery of Honour and tell your story in this legendary setting."
- url: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/about-collection-online
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19 (Rijksmuseum Collection Online launch page; no explicit publish date on page)
- confidence: high

### [8] Institutional object pages always label the work (title, artist, date, medium, inventory) and attach related/Discover modules — the industry object-detail pattern is large image + metadata + lateral browse, not a wordless lightbox.
- quote: "The Milkmaid / Johannes Vermeer, c. 1660 ... Artwork type painting / Object number SK-A-2344 ... ## Discover more / more by Johannes Vermeer"
- url: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/object/The-Milkmaid--42dd0e658c2979aec8e144d2357c55c0
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19
- confidence: high

### [9] Met object pages place the artwork image and title first, then structured “Artwork Details,” audio, and “More Artwork” facets (Related / By artist / In the same gallery / same medium / same time) — a rich lightbox analog with progressive disclosure, still fully labeled.
- quote: "Self-Portrait with a Straw Hat (obverse: The Potato Peeler) / Vincent van Gogh Dutch / 1887 / On view at The Met Fifth Avenue in Gallery 825 ... ## Artwork Details ... ## More Artwork / Related / By Vincent van Gogh / In the same gallery"
- url: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/436532
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19
- confidence: high

### [10] Louvre’s online collections database is institution/collection-info-first at the surface (500,000+ works, typology filters, themed albums) rather than a dark immersive gallery; dark immersive treatment is reserved for special VR/exhibition modes elsewhere, not default collection UI.
- quote: "The Collections database consists of entries for more than 500,000 works in the Musée du Louvre and Musée National Eugène-Delacroix. Updated on a daily basis..."
- url: https://collections.louvre.fr/en/
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19
- confidence: high

### [11] Live 2026 institutional collection/homepage UIs observed (Met, Tate, Rijksmuseum, Louvre, MoMA) use light/neutral institutional chrome; dark color appears mainly in exhibition marketing graphics (e.g., Met “Musical Bodies” dark purple, “Costume Art” black oval) and immersive VR, not as the default gallery wall color for collection browsing.
- quote: "[Musical Bodies] Graphic, designed on a dark purple background... [Costume Art] grayscale dress form on a black oval..." (exhibition cards) vs. standard light collection/home layouts
- url: https://www.metmuseum.org/
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19
- confidence: medium

### [12] MuseumNext (Dec 2024) frames wall labels as core interpretation — reveal, reframe, provoke — and cites Delaware Art Museum exit surveys where almost 8/10 visitors read community labels and ~1/3 said labels changed how they saw the photographs; professional practice assumes text beside works, opposite a fully wordless wall.
- quote: "exit surveys at DAM showed that almost eight out of ten visitors read these 'community contribution' labels. A third stated that reading them changed how they saw the photographs in the exhibition."
- url: https://www.museumnext.com/article/what-makes-a-great-museum-label/
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2024-12-01
- confidence: high

### [13] MuseumNext’s 2025 museum-experience trends (Jim Richardson, Jan 2025) prioritize belonging, interactivity, and digital–physical hybridity over pure institutional branding; the recommended online layer is engagement content (AR, streams, post-visit digital) rather than a static brand-first site.
- quote: "Craft a holistic engagement strategy that integrates on-site and online experiences seamlessly... Bridging the Digital-Physical Divide ... digital solutions continue to extend museums' reach."
- url: https://www.museumnext.com/article/from-loneliness-to-belonging-5-trends-are-hacking-the-museum-experience/
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2025-01-01
- confidence: medium

### [14] MuseumNext VR survey (Oct 2024) documents spatial/room metaphors as a recurring digital museum pattern (Musée d’Orsay Van Gogh drawing-room VR, Tate Modigliani studio recreation, Louvre “Mona Lisa: Beyond the Glass”), while quoting that physical objects remain the emotional core — supporting atmospheric gallery staging online as complement, not substitute.
- quote: "People are coming to a museum to see real objects because real objects are emotional." (Bruno David, via NYT, quoted by MuseumNext)
- url: https://www.museumnext.com/article/how-museums-are-using-virtual-reality/
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2024-10-10
- confidence: medium

### [15] Rijksmuseum Collection Online further layers participatory hanging (visitor stories/sets, user galleries, auto-generated theme pages, Linked Open Data open APIs, high-res free downloads) on top of the classic browse-grid — recent museum collection UX is interactive/curation-enabled, not only passive wall viewing.
- quote: "Through an innovative search bar and hundreds of automatically generated theme pages... create your own Collections... All freely available works can be downloaded in high resolution for both personal and commercial use."
- url: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/about-collection-online
- source_type: primary
- published: accessed 2026-09-19
- confidence: high
